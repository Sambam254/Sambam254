Cover Letter Writing Guide for Ontario

Write a cover letter that actually gets read.

By Samantha Russell · May 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Home › Careers Hub › Cover Letter Writing Guide for Ontario

Many job seekers treat the cover letter as an afterthought. That is a missed opportunity. A well-written cover letter is your chance to make a personal connection, address specific needs of the employer, and give context that a resume cannot.

Do Employers in Ontario Still Read Cover Letters?

Yes — when they ask for one. Many job postings in Ontario still request cover letters, and submitting a strong one when it is optional can differentiate you. When it is required, a weak or generic cover letter can eliminate an otherwise strong candidate.

The Structure of an Effective Cover Letter

Opening Paragraph: The Hook

Do not start with "I am writing to apply for..." Everyone does. Instead, open with something specific and compelling: why this particular company, what you know about their work, or your most relevant accomplishment. Show that you know who they are.

Middle Paragraph: The Match

Draw a direct line between the job requirements and your experience. Pick two or three of the most important requirements from the posting and address them specifically. Use concrete examples with numbers where possible. This is the core of your cover letter.

Closing Paragraph: The Ask

Express genuine enthusiasm for the role and company. State that you would welcome the opportunity to discuss your application further. Thank them for their time. Be confident, not apologetic.

Cover Letter Formatting in Canada

  • Length: maximum one page, three to four paragraphs
  • Font: same as your resume (Calibri, Arial, or Times New Roman, 10 to 12pt)
  • Address it to a specific person if possible — research the hiring manager's name on LinkedIn
  • Use standard business letter format with your contact information at the top

What to Avoid

  • Generic letters that could apply to any company
  • Simply restating your resume
  • Using "I" to start every sentence
  • Desperation: "I really need this job" or "I will accept any salary"
  • Typos — proofread carefully
