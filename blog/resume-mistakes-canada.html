Top Resume Mistakes Canadians Make

Avoid these common errors and get your resume noticed.

By Samantha Russell · May 7, 2026 · 7 min read

Home › Careers Hub › Top Resume Mistakes Canadians Make

A strong resume is your first impression with a potential employer. Unfortunately, many Canadian job seekers make avoidable mistakes that send their applications straight to the discard pile. Here are the most common ones and exactly how to fix them.

1. Including a Photo

Unlike some European countries, including a photo on a Canadian resume is not standard practice and may actually hurt your application. Canadian employers typically do not want photos to avoid perceptions of bias. Leave it off.

2. Including Personal Information

Do not include your date of birth, marital status, nationality, or Social Insurance Number on your resume. This information is not appropriate or required in Canada.

3. Using an Objective Statement Instead of a Profile Summary

Objective statements ("I am seeking a challenging role...") are outdated. Replace with a three-to-four line professional profile summary highlighting your key value to the employer.

4. Listing Duties Instead of Accomplishments

"Responsible for managing accounts" tells an employer nothing. "Managed 45 client accounts worth $2.3M annually, achieving 98% retention" tells them everything. Quantify wherever possible.

5. Using One Generic Resume for All Applications

Tailoring your resume to each job posting is essential, especially for ATS. Customize your skills section, profile summary, and even your bullet points to match the language of the specific job posting.

6. Inconsistent Formatting

Inconsistent fonts, bullet styles, spacing, or date formats look unprofessional and suggest poor attention to detail. Pick one format and apply it consistently throughout.

7. Making It Too Long

In Canada, most resumes should be one to two pages. Senior professionals with extensive experience may go to three pages, but longer is rarely better. Recruiters spend an average of six seconds on initial resume review.

8. Leaving Gaps Unexplained

Employment gaps are common and nothing to hide. Address them briefly in a cover letter if they are significant. Some candidates list relevant activities during gaps (volunteering, freelancing, caregiving, education).

9. Not Proofreading

Typos and grammatical errors signal carelessness. Always proofread carefully. Read it aloud. Have someone else review it. Use grammar tools as a secondary check.
