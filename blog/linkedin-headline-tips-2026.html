How to Write a LinkedIn Headline That Gets Recruiters to Click

The formula used for every client, and why most people get this completely wrong.

Quick Answers

What should my LinkedIn headline say? Include your role, specialty, location or target region, and 1 to 2 searchable keywords. Never waste it on just your job title. LinkedIn gives you 220 characters and most people use fewer than 40.

Does the headline affect recruiter searches? Yes directly. LinkedIn ranks profiles based on keyword relevance in your headline, summary, and experience. An optimized headline dramatically increases how often you appear in recruiter searches.

Should I put Open to Work in my headline? Use "Open to [Role Type] Roles" rather than "Seeking Opportunities." Also enable the LinkedIn Open to Work feature which signals recruiters directly. Use both together for maximum visibility.

How often should I update my LinkedIn headline? Every 6 months at minimum, or whenever you earn a new credential, change your target role, or shift your career focus.

Your LinkedIn headline is the first thing a recruiter sees when they search for candidates. It appears under your name in every search result, connection request, and comment you leave. It is 220 characters that either make a recruiter click your profile or keep scrolling.

Most people waste it by writing their current job title and nothing else. That is the single most common LinkedIn mistake I see as an HR recruiter who actively searches for candidates on the platform.

Your LinkedIn headline is not your job title. It is your 220-character pitch to every recruiter searching for someone with your skills right now.

Why Your Current Headline Is Probably Not Working

When a recruiter searches LinkedIn for candidates, they use keywords to find profiles. LinkedIn ranks profiles based on how well your headline, summary, and experience match those keywords. A headline that just says "Customer Service Representative at XYZ Company" is not optimized for search and tells a recruiter nothing about what you are capable of.

LinkedIn gives you 220 characters for your headline. The default setting fills it in with your current job title automatically. Almost nobody changes it. That is your opportunity to stand out.

The Recruiter Formula for LinkedIn Headlines

Here is the structure used when rewriting LinkedIn profiles for clients:

[What you do] + [Who you do it for or where] + [Your strongest differentiator or goal] + [1 to 2 keywords]

The 5 Rules for a High-Performing LinkedIn Headline

Rule 1: Use Keywords Recruiters Actually Search

Think about what a recruiter would type into LinkedIn to find someone like you. Those words belong in your headline. Use job titles, skills, software names, industry terms, and certifications. Check 5 job postings in your target field and note the words that appear most often.

Rule 2: Include Your Location or Target Region

Recruiters in Ontario search by location. Including "Ontario," "GTA," "Durham Region," or your city increases the likelihood that local recruiters find you. This is especially important if you are targeting a specific market.

Rule 3: State Your Value, Not Just Your Title

What do you do that makes you valuable? What results do you produce? If you manage teams, say so. If you have a specific certification, include it. A title tells a recruiter what you are called. A value statement tells them what you can do.

Rule 4: Signal Availability Strategically

If you are actively looking, include "Open to [Role Type] Roles" rather than "Seeking Opportunities" which reads as passive. LinkedIn also has an Open to Work feature that signals recruiters directly. Use both.

Rule 5: Update It Every 6 Months

Your career evolves. Your headline should too. Whenever you gain a new certification, take on new responsibilities, or shift your target role, update your headline. A fresh headline with current keywords keeps you visible.

Quick Win

Right now, open LinkedIn, click Edit on your profile, and look at your current headline. If it is just your job title, you have 220 characters of free real estate doing nothing for you. Change it today using the formula above and watch your profile views increase within the week.

What Happens When Recruiters Find Your Profile

A strong headline gets the click. But once a recruiter opens your profile, your About section needs to hold their attention. Open with a single punchy sentence about who you are and what you do. Follow it with your top three to five career accomplishments in plain language. End with a clear call to action.
