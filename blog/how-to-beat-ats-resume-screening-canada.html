How to Beat ATS Resume Screening in Canada

Get your resume past the robots and in front of real hiring managers.

By Samantha Russell · May 5, 2026 · 8 min read

Home › Careers Hub › How to Beat ATS Resume Screening in Canada

Most Canadian employers with structured hiring processes use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to screen resumes before a human ever sees them. If your resume is not optimized for ATS, you may be eliminated before your application is even read — no matter how qualified you are.

What Is an ATS?

An Applicant Tracking System is software that receives, stores, and filters resumes based on criteria set by the employer. The system scans for keywords, formatting compatibility, and relevant experience. Resumes that do not meet the criteria are ranked low or automatically filtered out.

Why ATS Matters in Canada

Major Canadian employers including banks, government departments, healthcare systems, and large corporations all use ATS. Even mid-size companies frequently use platforms like Workday, Greenhouse, Taleo, Lever, and iCIMS.

How to Optimize Your Resume for ATS

1. Use Keywords from the Job Posting

Read the job posting carefully. Identify the key skills, qualifications, and phrases the employer uses — then mirror that language in your resume. If the posting says "stakeholder management," use those exact words, not "relationship building."

2. Use Standard Section Headings

Stick to conventional headings: Work Experience, Education, Skills, Certifications. Creative headings like "My Journey" or "What I Bring" confuse ATS parsers.

3. Use a Clean, Simple Format

Avoid tables, columns, headers/footers, text boxes, and graphics. ATS systems often cannot parse these correctly. Use a single-column format with clean fonts (Calibri, Arial, Times New Roman).

4. Submit as a .docx or .pdf (Check the Posting)

Most modern ATS systems handle both .docx and .pdf. If the job posting does not specify, .docx is generally safer for parsing. Never submit a scanned image of your resume.

5. Include a Skills Section

A dedicated skills section gives the ATS a clear place to find your technical and soft skills. List them clearly: "Project Management, Budget Forecasting, Stakeholder Engagement, Microsoft Excel."

6. Do Not Keyword Stuff

Adding keywords in white text or at excessive density will flag your resume as spam. Use keywords naturally, in context, where they genuinely describe your experience.

After ATS: What Matters to Humans

Once your resume clears ATS, a recruiter or hiring manager reads it. At that point, clarity, relevance, and accomplishments matter more than keywords. Quantify your achievements: "Managed a team of 12 and reduced project delivery time by 20%."
